← Work / Case study 02 · Traveloka

Testing which words move people from browsing to installing

Before AI, my discipline for judging language was experimentation: write distinct hypotheses, put them in front of real traffic, and read the results honestly. This is where that habit started.

Role

UX Writer

Period

2019–2021

Scope

App-download copy variants and testing

Markets

Southeast Asia, incl. Thailand & Singapore

01 — Hypotheses

Three ways to say “get the app”

Each variant tested a different reason to install, rather than three rewordings of the same idea.

different reasons, not rewrites

Variant A · Speed

Book in a few taps

Your details are saved, so checkout takes seconds.

Get the app

Variant B · Value

App-only deals tonight

Some prices are only available in the app.

See app deals

Variant C · Control

Your whole trip, one place

E-tickets, vouchers and changes — even offline.

Download free

Illustrative concept — pending confirmation

02 — Reported results

What the campaign report showed

Figures as reported in the original campaign document at the time.

reported ≠ proven uplift

Thailand · Clicks

301,221

Reported clicks during the campaign period.

Thailand · Installs

15,482

Reported installs during the same period.

Singapore · CVR

7.30%

Reported conversion rate.

Reading these honestly

Historical campaign figures, not current performance. No baseline or control is reported, so they are not presented as uplift, and results can’t be attributed to copy alone.

04 — Reflection

What carried forward

Treat every line as a hypothesis, and be precise about what the evidence does and doesn’t prove. It’s the same discipline I now bring to evaluating AI output.
