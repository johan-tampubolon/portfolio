Designing recommendations merchants can act on

Inside the GrabMerchant AI Assistant, recommendations turn live performance data into a next step. I designed the language system underneath: the intent map, response logic, the system prompt that governs voice and guardrails, and contributions to how recommendations and generated content are evaluated.

Role

Senior Content Designer — UX writer on the assistant

I owned

Conversation logic, voice & tone, system prompt

I contributed to

Evaluation frameworks: recommendations and generated content

Partners

Product, data science, AI & engineering

The problem

Rich data. No way to make it useful.

Small business owners on GrabMerchant struggled to improve performance. They were time-poor, overwhelmed, and unsure where to start.

What merchants kept asking

Why are my sales going down?

What should I do to get more orders?

How do I even start optimizing?

The opportunity: an assistant that answers in natural language, surfaces personalized insights, converts them into low-effort actions — and earns trust through calm, clear language.

01 — Intent map

Mapping the messy into meaningful intent

We sifted support tickets, bot queries, search logs and interviews to find the patterns beneath the noise.

messy questions first, words second

Growing sales Fixing operations Managing menus Learning from peers

Raw query

“Why am I not getting orders?”

Growing sales → diagnose with order and visibility signals before advising.

Coach, not search engine

The assistant had to feel like it understood the business before the merchant finished the question.

02 — System prompt

Writing the rules the model speaks by

I wrote the system prompt that governs how the assistant speaks and responds. Prompt guidance shapes behavior — it is not a guarantee of enforcement.

hedge, don’t guess

  5. 5
    BoundariesOut-of-scope topics hand off to Support

System prompt — excerpt

## Uncertainty
If the data covers fewer than 7 days, say so.
Describe what changed; do not claim why.
✓ “Orders dipped 12% vs last week. Photos
   on top dishes often help — want to try?”
✗ “Your sales dropped because of competitors.”

## Boundaries
Payouts, disputes, policy → hand off:
“That’s one for our Support team — here’s
 how to reach them.”

03 — Response logic

Tie every suggestion to a signal, not a vibe

Before the model, there was a doc full of “What should it say if…?”. Logic trees became modular, templated content blocks.

Testing with merchants: they liked clarity over cleverness, flagged advice that sounded robotic or vague, and wanted suggestions — not just answers. We refined copy, restructured decision trees and added fallbacks.

04 — Evaluation

Two kinds of “good” — judged separately
